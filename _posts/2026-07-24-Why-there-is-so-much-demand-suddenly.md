title: "Why there is so much demand suddenly"
author: "asheesh"
---

When I joined the plant in April 2026, life looked easy. There was demand, but it was manageable in general shift only, and things went smoothly in May and especially in June, where we mostly met demand very easily. But in July, we are struggling, and it seems demand is at least double our capacity to supply. One more thing is that demand for products which were rarely in demand over the last two months suddenly burst out. Take the example of transformer oil: most of the time only barrels were required, but now in the last 10 days we have bulk requirements of at least twice our capacity for producing it, creating a growing list of backlogs.

I was thinking why there is a sudden demand in lubricants—especially bulk, not packed—but not in petrol and diesel, and what is unseen that I could not see at first. I wondered if it was prices or something else, so I looked at the crude prices. One thing is clear: there was a downward trajectory of crude in June, but then there was a reversal of this trend. In India, lubrication operates on dynamic pricing where market prices are not fixed. Because of this, there was a sense in May and June that crude prices would fall at one point. Lubrication is not a product you consume just today; it is an elastic product, meaning if prices are high, demand falls rapidly as buyers shift demand to future months. So when prices started falling, there was a downward price revision and people started buying. However, in less than a 10-day cycle, a reversal happened and there grew a sense that prices would increase again, driving people to buy as much as possible within that window of arbitrage.

Petrol and diesel demand did not respond that way because their prices are not determined directly by the market in the same manner. Everyone knows a hundred-dollar crude price does not immediately change petrol and diesel pump prices. Furthermore, petrol and diesel are inelastic products, meaning if prices increase, demand does not change much because people still have to go to the office.

Someone may ask why packed lubricant demand did not appear as high. Packed demand was actually high as well, but because inventory at regional nodes was at an all-time high, it did not show up directly on the plant floor. You cannot put bulk into inventory at CIP or CFA locations; bulk is a plant-to-mouth product, which is why all of these market dynamics reflect directly at the plant level.

The demand we are getting now is what is called pent-up demand. It is showing up now because high prices artificially curtailed demand over the last four months. One thing we missed, but can plan better for next time, is that these cycles can be predicted. We can plan for pent-up demand by building up bulk inventory at sufficient levels ahead of time, just like we do for packed products.
